# Journal of Experiments — candidate entries

The backlog of experiments to write about. Collected 2026-10-18; kept here so whoever picks up the next entry,
person or agent, can find it. How to write one is in `AGENTS.md` at the root of this repo.

## How to use this list

Every idea is one line that begins with its status in brackets. The status is the only thing that changes, and it
changes on the idea's own line, so that several people can claim ideas at once without their commits colliding.
Never move a line between sections.

- `[open]` — nobody has it. Prefer one with strong primary sources (a government report, a diary, a court record).
- `[claimed: <name>, <date>]` — someone is writing it. Set this first, commit it and push it to `main` before you
  start, so nobody else does the same work.
- `[published: <path>]` — done; the path is the entry. Add anything you found that belongs on this list as a new
  `[open]` line.

Two ideas are marked `suggested next`; take one of those unless you have a reason to take another.

The entries share a shape: someone builds a thing or seals a system to test one hypothesis, the instrument speaks,
and the lesson is about whether anyone had a procedure for listening, or whether they were reading the right
instrument at all. The ideas are grouped by which published entry they would sit beside.

## Craft that killed their makers

*Beside "Dive Eighty-Eight"*

- [open] **The H. L. Hunley, 1863–64** — A hand-cranked submarine that drowned two crews in trials, including its namesake, then sank the *Housatonic* and never surfaced. A weapon that killed more of its own than the enemy's.
- [open] **R101, 1930** — Britain's state-built airship, flown to India on a minister's deadline with its full trials skipped; 48 dead at Beauvais. Its privately built twin, R100, had just flown to Canada and back.
- [open] **Langley's Aerodrome, 1903** — The government-funded, scientist-led flying machine goes into the Potomac twice; nine days after the second time, two bicycle mechanics fly.
- [open] **Sealab III, 1969** — The Navy's saturation-diving habitat; aquanaut Berry Cannon dies on the first descent with an empty CO₂ scrubber in his rig. The programme ends there.
- [open] **The Italia, 1928** — Nobile's airship crashes on the pack ice north of Spitsbergen on the way back from the Pole; the "red tent" survivors, Malmgren's death on the ice, Amundsen lost in the search. A sealer looking for Nobile's party in July 1930 walked over Andrée's camp on White Island without seeing it (Andrée's Story, 1930, p. 199).

## Sealed systems and solitary subjects

*Beside "Two Years Under Glass"*

- [open] **Michel Siffre, 1962 and 1972** — A geologist lives in caves without clocks; his body settles into 48-hour days. The subject's own sense of time was the instrument.
- [open] **Byrd at Advance Base, 1934** — The admiral winters alone in a buried hut and slowly poisons himself on his stove's carbon monoxide while refusing to call for help.
- [open] **The Minnesota Starvation Experiment, 1944–45** — 36 conscientious objectors half-starved for six months so the Allies could learn to refeed Europe; what it found about the mind under hunger still stands.
- [open] **BIOS-3, Krasnoyarsk, 1972 on** — The Soviet closed ecosystem that mostly worked, as the counterexample to Biosphere 2. The companion piece *Biosphere 1* already gives it a page, so this would go further.
- [open] **Camp Century, 1959–67** — A nuclear reactor under the Greenland ice; the tunnels crushed in a decade, and the waste left behind is now expected to melt out.

## One person, one hypothesis, nobody watching

*Beside "The Nuclear Boy Scout"*

- [open, suggested next] **Louis Slotin and the demon core, 1946** — A criticality assembly held apart by a screwdriver; a procedure built around one steady hand, used until the hand slipped. The same core had already killed Harry Daghlian.
- [open] **Alain Bombard, 1952** — Crosses the Atlantic in an inflatable with no provisions to prove castaways can live on fish and sips of seawater; survives, and the argument about whether he proved it never ended.
- [open] **Max von Pettenkofer, 1892** — Drinks a flask of cholera to disprove Koch, gets mild diarrhoea, and declares germ theory refuted. A lucky result read as a true one.
- [open] **Duncan MacDougall, 1907** — Weighs six dying patients on a balance bed; one gives a number, and "21 grams" outlives all the data.
- [open] **Charles Hatfield, 1916** — San Diego hires a rainmaker; the dam breaks; the city refuses to pay because paying would admit it caused the flood.
- [open] **The dolphin house, 1965** — John Lilly floods a house so Margaret Howe Lovatt can live with a dolphin and teach it English.
- [open] **Glaisher and Coxwell, 1862** — The meteorologist James Glaisher takes a balloon to about 9,000 metres over Wolverhampton to read instruments and loses consciousness; Coxwell, hands frozen, opens the valve with his teeth. Glaisher's own reports to the British Association are the record. (Andrée, bumping across the ice on 14 July 1897, wondered if a returning pigeon had "done the same as Glaisher's pigeon.")

## Results nobody read

*The site's recurring lesson*

- [open] **James Lind, 1747** — The first controlled trial finds citrus cures scurvy; the Navy acts 48 years later, and Lind himself never quite believed it.
- [claimed: Claude (Mincome session), 2026-10-19] **Mincome and SIME/DIME, 1970s** — Guaranteed-income trials; the Manitoba data sat boxed for 30 years, and the US one was killed by a divorce finding later shown to be an artefact.
- [open] **Cambridge-Somerville, 1939–45** — A mentoring programme for at-risk boys; thirty years on, the boys who got the help had done worse. Joan McCord's follow-up is the whole entry.
- [open] **Carrel's chicken heart, 1912–46** — Cells kept "immortal" for 34 years; after his death nobody could repeat it, and the likeliest reason is that the feed kept adding fresh cells.
- [open] **Kon-Tiki, 1947** — The raft reaches Polynesia and proves the voyage possible; the hypothesis it was built to prove turns out to be wrong.
- [open] **Universe 25, 1968–72** — Calhoun's mouse utopia collapses; what he measured and what the public took from it are two different experiments.
- [open] **St. Matthew Island, 1944–66** — 29 reindeer put ashore become 6,000, then 42. No one set out to test carrying capacity, but nothing has tested it better. *Sixteen Reindeer* tells it in a paragraph; link to that.

## Whole systems run as trials

- [open] **Fordlandia, 1928–45** — Michigan transplanted to the Amazon; the rubber trees planted in rows catch the blight that scattered wild trees never do.
- [open] **The Groundnut Scheme, 1947–51** — Britain farms peanuts across Tanganyika without surveying the soil or the rainfall; tens of millions lost.
- [open] **Project Cybersyn, 1971–73** — Stafford Beer wires Chile's factories to an operations room by telex; it works through a national strike and dies with the coup.
- [open] **Wörgl, 1932–33** — An Austrian town issues money that loses value monthly; unemployment falls; the central bank shuts it down. (Close to Captain's Notes territory, so it may be a crossover.)
- [open] **Starfish Prime and West Ford, 1961–63** — A 1.4-megaton test that put out Honolulu's streetlights and crippled a third of the satellites in orbit; half a billion copper needles flown as an artificial ionosphere.
- [open] **Chernobyl's rundown test, 1986** — The disaster was itself a safety experiment, run at low power after a grid dispatcher delayed it into the night shift.
