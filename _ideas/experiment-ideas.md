# Journal of Experiments — candidate entries

The backlog of experiments to write about. Collected 2026-10-18; kept here so whoever picks up the next entry,
person or agent, can find it. How to write one is in `AGENTS.md` at the root of this repo.

## How to use this list

Every idea is one line that begins with its status in brackets. The status is the only thing that changes, and it
changes on the idea's own line, so that several people can claim ideas at once without their commits colliding.
Never move a line between sections.

- `[open]` — nobody has it. Prefer one with strong primary sources (a government report, a diary, a court record).
- `[claimed: <name>, <date>]` — someone is writing it. Set this first, commit it and push it to `main` before you
  start, so nobody else does the same work.
- `[published: <path>]` — done; the path is the entry. Add anything you found that belongs on this list as a new
  `[open]` line.

Two ideas are marked `suggested next`; take one of those unless you have a reason to take another.

The entries share a shape: someone builds a thing or seals a system to test one hypothesis, the instrument speaks,
and the lesson is about whether anyone had a procedure for listening, or whether they were reading the right
instrument at all. The ideas are grouped by which published entry they would sit beside.

## Craft that killed their makers

*Beside "Dive Eighty-Eight"*

- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-two-crews.md] **The H. L. Hunley, 1863–64** — A hand-cranked submarine that drowned two crews in trials, including its namesake, then sank the *Housatonic* and never surfaced. A weapon that killed more of its own than the enemy's.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-four-thousand-pads.md] **R101, 1930** — Britain's state-built airship, flown to India on a minister's deadline with its full trials skipped; 48 dead at Beauvais. Its privately built twin, R100, had just flown to Canada and back.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-nine-days.md] **Langley's Aerodrome, 1903** — The government-funded, scientist-led flying machine goes into the Potomac twice; nine days after the second time, two bicycle mechanics fly.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-six-hundred-feet.md] **Sealab III, 1969** — The Navy's saturation-diving habitat; aquanaut Berry Cannon dies on the first descent with an empty CO₂ scrubber in his rig. The programme ends there.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-four-hundred-telegrams.md] **The Italia, 1928** — Nobile's airship crashes on the pack ice north of Spitsbergen on the way back from the Pole; the "red tent" survivors, Malmgren's death on the ice, Amundsen lost in the search. A sealer looking for Nobile's party in July 1930 walked over Andrée's camp on White Island without seeing it (Andrée's Story, 1930, p. 199).
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-fifty-four-tests.md] **Apollo 1, 1967** — The "plugs-out" test on the pad, rated non-hazardous, in pure oxygen at 16 pounds per square inch; three men dead in seventeen seconds, in a test that had been run before. NASA's Apollo 204 Review Board report and the Senate hearings are US government works.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-nine-hundred-north.md] **USS Thresher, 1963** — A deep-dive test off Cape Cod; 129 dead; the silver-brazed joints that had failed their own ultrasonic tests and the ballast-blow valves that iced. The Navy's court of inquiry was declassified in 2020 to 2021: thousands of pages, public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-three-planks.md] **The Vasa, 1628** — The stability test (thirty men running from side to side on the deck) that the admiral stopped after three runs, and the ship that sailed anyway and went over in her first mile. The inquest's transcripts survive; the Vasa Museum has published them.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-eighteen-thousand.md] **The Comet, 1954** — De Havilland's jet airliner, the first in service, lost two aircraft in the sea off Elba and Naples within three months. The Royal Aircraft Establishment put a third Comet in a water tank at Farnborough and pressurised it until it split at the corner of a window, and found that the fatigue test run before service had been made on a section already proof-loaded to twice the working pressure, which had strengthened the very corners that failed. The court of inquiry's report (HMSO, 1955) is Crown copyright, expired; the RAE's reports are in the National Archives. Beside *Four Thousand Pads*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-twenty-five-hundred.md] **The Eastland, 1915** — A Chicago excursion steamer, top-heavy from the day she was launched and warned about in writing in 1913, was fitted with extra lifeboats and rafts under the Seamen's Act passed after the Titanic; on 24 July 1915, tied to the dock in the Chicago River with 2,500 aboard, she rolled onto her side and 844 drowned. The federal inquiry, the grand jury record and the Chicago papers of 1915 (archive.org) are public domain. Beside *Three Planks*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-two-and-a-quarter-inches.md] **The Quebec Bridge, 1907** — The longest cantilever span ever attempted went up over the St Lawrence with its dead weight never recalculated after the span was lengthened; in August 1907 the inspector found a lower chord bending, the consulting engineer Theodore Cooper wired from New York to add no more load, and the message was still on its way when the south arm fell on 29 August with 75 men on it. The Royal Commission's report (1908) is Canadian Crown copyright, expired, with the inspector's notes and Cooper's testimony. Beside *One in Seventy-Two*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-ship-twenty-nine.md] **The DC-10 cargo door, 1972 to 1974** — A door that could be latched without being locked: the floor of an American Airlines DC-10 collapsed over Windsor, Ontario, in June 1972 and the crew landed it; the NTSB asked for a mandatory fix, the FAA and McDonnell Douglas settled on a voluntary one, and in March 1974 the same door opened on a Turkish Airlines DC-10 whose fix had been recorded as done, near Paris, with 346 dead. The NTSB's Windsor report (1973), the French commission's report (1976) and the House hearings of 1974 are open or public domain. Beside *Four Thousand Pads*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-fourteen-hundred-pounds.md] **The negative pressure test, 2010** — On the Deepwater Horizon on 20 April 2010 the well was tested by bleeding its pressure off and watching: the drill pipe showed 1,400 pounds per square inch that should have been zero, the crew explained it as a "bladder effect", called the test a success, and the well blew out that night with eleven dead. The National Commission's report and its Chief Counsel's report (2011) and the joint Coast Guard and Bureau investigation are US government works, with the rig's data traces and the testimony. Beside *Dive Eighty-Eight*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-three-inches.md] **The St Francis Dam, 1928** — William Mulholland's dam above Los Angeles, raised twice during construction without widening its base, leaked from the day it filled; he inspected the leaks on the morning of 12 March 1928 and called them normal, and it failed that midnight, killing more than four hundred down the Santa Clara valley. The coroner's inquest transcript and the Governor's commission report (1928) are public domain. Beside *Seven Hundred Metres*.
- [open] **HMS Captain, 1866 to 1870** — Captain Cowper Coles's low-freeboard turret ship, pressed on the Admiralty through Parliament and the press against its Chief Constructor Edward Reed, and built by Laird so far over her designed weight that she floated with six feet seven inches of freeboard instead of eight; an inclining experiment at Portsmouth in July 1870 gave the curve of stability that showed how little heel she could bear, and it was worked up while she was at sea; she capsized in a squall off Cape Finisterre on the night of 6 to 7 September 1870 with Coles and about 480 men, and 18 survived. The court martial (1870), the Admiralty papers laid before Parliament and The Times are public domain. Beside *Three Planks* and *Two Crews*.
- [open] **Otto Lilienthal, 1891 to 1896** — Some two thousand glides from the hill he built at Lichterfelde and from the Rhinow hills, on the hypothesis that a man must learn to balance in the air before he fits an engine; his book Der Vogelflug als Grundlage der Fliegekunst (1889; English 1911) and Ottomar Anschütz's photographs are public domain; a stall at the Gollenberg on 9 August 1896 broke his spine and he died the next day. The Wrights read him. Beside *Nine Days*.
- [open] **The welded ships, 1941 to 1947** — Of about 2,700 Liberty ships and the welded tankers built beside them, hundreds cracked and a dozen or so broke in two, the tanker Schenectady snapping across at her fitting-out pier at Portland, Oregon, on 16 January 1943 in still water; the Board of Investigation sat from 1943, and Constance Tipper's notch tests at Cambridge showed the steel turned brittle a little below room temperature. The Board's final report (Government Printing Office, 1947) is public domain. Beside *Eighteen Thousand*.
- [open] **The molasses tank, 1915 to 1919** — United States Industrial Alcohol's steel tank on Commercial Street, Boston, 50 feet high and 90 across, was tested before its first filling with six inches of water, leaked from the day it took molasses and was painted brown to hide the stains; it burst on 15 January 1919 and the wave killed 21 people. The auditor Hugh Ogden's findings in the civil suits (1925) and the Boston Globe of 1919 are public domain, with photographs at the Boston Public Library. Beside *Three Inches* and *Two and a Quarter Inches*.
- [open] **The Tay Bridge, 1871 to 1880** — Thomas Bouch's two-mile bridge over the Firth of Tay was designed for a wind pressure of ten pounds a square foot on the Astronomer Royal's word, carried no wind gauge, and lost its high girders with a train on the night of 28 December 1879, about 75 dead; the Court of Inquiry's report (1880), with the evidence of the inspectors, the foundry and the Astronomer Royal, is public domain and full text on archive.org. The site's third bridge. Beside *One in Seventy-Two* and *Two and a Quarter Inches*.

## Sealed systems and solitary subjects

*Beside "Two Years Under Glass"*

- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-twenty-five-days.md] **Michel Siffre, 1962 and 1972** — A geologist lives in caves without clocks; his body settles into 48-hour days. The subject's own sense of time was the instrument.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-seventy-eight-below.md] **Byrd at Advance Base, 1934** — The admiral winters alone in a buried hut and slowly poisons himself on his stove's carbon monoxide while refusing to call for help.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-three-menus.md] **The Minnesota Starvation Experiment, 1944–45** — 36 conscientious objectors half-starved for six months so the Allies could learn to refeed Europe; what it found about the mind under hunger still stands.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-ninety-one-percent.md] **BIOS-3, Krasnoyarsk, 1972 on** — The Soviet closed ecosystem that mostly worked, as the counterexample to Biosphere 2. The companion piece *Biosphere 1* already gives it a page, so this would go further.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-thirty-inches.md] **Camp Century, 1959–67** — A nuclear reactor under the Greenland ice; the tunnels crushed in a decade, and the waste left behind is now expected to melt out.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-eighteen-g.md] **Stapp's rocket sled, 1954** — A colonel rides a sled to 632 miles an hour and stops in 1.4 seconds, 46 g, with his own body as the instrument; his reading of his own data produced the seat belt. Air Force reports, films and photographs from Holloman, all US government works.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-the-right-glove.md] **Kittinger's Excelsior jumps, 1959–60** — A balloon to 102,800 feet and a fall of four and a half minutes; on the way up his right glove lost pressure and he chose not to report it. Air Force material, public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-fifteen-sledges.md] **Wegener at Eismitte, 1930–31** — Three men wintering in a cave in the ice at the centre of Greenland, the propeller sledges that failed, the supply arithmetic nobody re-did, and Wegener's walk back in November and death on the ice. His diaries are public domain (he died in 1930); the companions' accounts and Georgi's *Mid-Ice* are not; the expedition's scientific results (1933 to 1940) and the German press of 1930 to 1931 on the Deutsches Zeitungsportal are open.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-four-million-minutes.md] **Mars-500, 2010–11** — Six men sealed for 520 days in a module in Moscow for a pretend flight to Mars; the actigraphs on their wrists showed most of them going torpid, one losing his 24-hour day, one hardly sleeping, and nobody watching the video could tell. Basner and others, *PNAS* 2013, is open access; ESA's and the IBMP's own material.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-eighty-five-goats.md] **Haldane's goats, 1906 to 1908** — The Admiralty asked J. S. Haldane how divers could go deeper than the tables allowed; he took goats to the Lister Institute, worked out that the body tolerates a halving of pressure without the bends, and tested staged decompression on the goats and then on Lieutenant Damant and Gunner Catto to 210 feet in Loch Striven. Boycott, Damant and Haldane in the Journal of Hygiene (1908) and the Admiralty committee's report (1907) are public domain. Beside *Six Hundred Feet*.
- [open] **Tektite, 1969 to 1970** (overnight: the text is written on branch `worktree-agent-ae2fb1e1f7614eb32`; it needs a date, the checker, a pull request and narration: see RUN-LOG) — Four scientists lived 60 days in a habitat at 50 feet in Lameshur Bay, St John, for the Navy, NASA and the Interior Department, who wanted to watch a crew in isolation as much as the reef; Tektite II in 1970 ran ten crews, one of them all women under Sylvia Earle. The programme's reports are US government works, and the behavioural observations were the point. Beside *Six Hundred Feet* and *Two Years Under Glass*.
- [open] **The Lunar-Mars Life Support Test Project, 1997** — NASA sealed four people for 91 days in a chamber at Johnson Space Center from September to December 1997, with wheat and a bioreactor making their air and water and the mass balances published; the counterpart to Biosphere 2 and BIOS-3 that nobody outside NASA read. The reports are on NTRS, public domain. Beside *Two Years Under Glass* and *Ninety-One Percent*.
- [open] **Kleitman in Mammoth Cave, 1938** — The sleep researcher Nathaniel Kleitman and his student Bruce Richardson lived 32 days in Mammoth Cave on a 28-hour day to see whether the body's temperature rhythm would follow the clock; Richardson's did and Kleitman's would not. Sleep and Wakefulness (1939) is in copyright (check the renewal record); the newspapers of July 1938 and Kleitman's papers at Chicago are the record. Beside *Twenty-Five Days*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-a-pair-of-oilskin-breeches.md] **The Jeannette and the Fram, 1879 to 1896** — James Gordon Bennett's Jeannette sailed through Bering Strait to test Petermann's theory of a warm current and an open sea at the Pole, was frozen in for two years and crushed, and De Long and nineteen others died on the Lena delta; when her wreckage turned up on the Greenland coast three years later, Nansen read the drift and built the Fram to be frozen in on purpose. The Navy's court of inquiry (1883), De Long's journals (1883) and Farthest North (1897) are public domain. Beside *Sixty-Five Hours*.

## One person, one hypothesis, nobody watching

*Beside "The Nuclear Boy Scout"*

- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-the-demon-core.md] **Louis Slotin and the demon core, 1946** — A criticality assembly held apart by a screwdriver; a procedure built around one steady hand, used until the hand slipped. The same core had already killed Harry Daghlian.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-one-day-in-sixty-five.md] **Alain Bombard, 1952** — Crosses the Atlantic in an inflatable with no provisions to prove castaways can live on fish and sips of seawater; survives, and the argument about whether he proved it never ended.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-one-cubic-centimetre.md] **Max von Pettenkofer, 1892** — Drinks a flask of cholera to disprove Koch, gets mild diarrhoea, and declares germ theory refuted. A lucky result read as a true one.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-two-silver-dollars.md] **Duncan MacDougall, 1907** — Weighs six dying patients on a balance bed; one gives a number, and "21 grams" outlives all the data.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-four-billion-gallons.md] **Charles Hatfield, 1916** — San Diego hires a rainmaker; the dam breaks; the city refuses to pay because paying would admit it caused the flood.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-one-clear-word.md] **The dolphin house, 1965** — John Lilly floods a house so Margaret Howe Lovatt can live with a dolphin and teach it English.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-seven-miles.md] **Glaisher and Coxwell, 1862** — The meteorologist James Glaisher takes a balloon to about 9,000 metres over Wolverhampton to read instruments and loses consciousness; Coxwell, hands frozen, opens the valve with his teeth. Glaisher's own reports to the British Association are the record. (Andrée, bumping across the ice on 14 July 1897, wondered if a returning pigeon had "done the same as Glaisher's pigeon.")
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-building-number-one.md] **Camp Lazear, 1900** — Walter Reed's two huts in Cuba, one filled with the soiled bedding of the dead, one with mosquitoes, and volunteers in each; Lazear dead before the result. The Reed commission's reports, the Senate documents of 1911 and the Hench collection at the University of Virginia are open.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-eleven-convicts.md] **Goldberger's pellagra, 1915–16** — Eleven Mississippi convicts fed the Southern diet until they sickened, then the "filth parties" in which Goldberger, his wife and his assistants swallowed the scrapings of the sick to prove it was not infectious; the South refused the result for twenty years. Public Health Service reports, public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-an-aluminium-prism.md] **N-rays, 1903–04** — Blondlot's new radiation, confirmed in dozens of French papers, until Wood pocketed the prism in the dark and the readings went on as before. The Comptes rendus and Wood's letter to Nature (1904), public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-a-dozen-healthy-infants.md] **Little Albert, 1920** — Watson and Rayner's one infant, taught to fear a white rat; the famous result never repeated, and the century-long hunt for who the child was. The 1920 paper and the film are public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-five-drops.md] **Pasteur at Pouilly-le-Fort, 1881** — A public trial on fifty sheep: the vaccinated live, the others die on the day Pasteur named; the vaccine was made by a rival's method, which his notebooks record and his reports do not. The *Comptes rendus* of 1880 to 1881 (archive.org; Gallica wants a human); the notebooks are digitised at the BnF; Geison (1995) is in copyright.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-sixty-five-centimetres.md] **Forssmann, 1929** — A surgical resident at Eberswalde threads a urinary catheter 65 centimetres up a vein in his own arm into his right atrium, walks to the X-ray room to prove it, and publishes in Klinische Wochenschrift in November 1929; he is dismissed from the Charité, turns to urology, and gets the Nobel Prize for it in 1956. The 1929 paper is a pre-1930 publication, public domain; his memoir (1972) is not. Beside *One Cubic Centimetre*.
- [open] **Stubbins Ffirth, 1804** — A Philadelphia medical student sets out to prove yellow fever is not contagious by rubbing the black vomit of the dying into cuts in his arms, dropping it in his eyes and drinking it; he does not sicken, publishes his thesis, and is right for the wrong reason, the disease passing only through the mosquito and his samples coming from patients past the days when their blood could carry it. A Treatise on Malignant Fever (1804) is public domain. Beside *One Cubic Centimetre* and *Building Number One*.
- [open] **Carrión, 1885** — A Lima medical student has himself inoculated with blood from a verruga wart to prove that Peruvian wart and Oroya fever are one disease; he develops Oroya fever and dies on 5 October 1885, and the disease carries his name. His fellow students' account (1886) and the Lima medical journals of 1885 are in Spanish and public domain. Beside *The Demon Core* and *Building Number One*.
- [open] **Marshall, 1984** — Barry Marshall, unable to infect piglets with the bacterium he and Robin Warren had found in ulcer patients, drinks a culture of it, has gastritis within a week and clears it with antibiotics; the profession takes a decade to agree. The mirror of Pettenkofer: a true result that was not believed. The 1984 Lancet papers and his 1985 report in the Medical Journal of Australia are in copyright, quoted briefly; his Nobel lecture (2005) is open. Beside *One Cubic Centimetre*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-case-seventeen.md] **Jenner, 1796** — On 14 May 1796 Edward Jenner scratches matter from a milkmaid's cowpox into the arm of James Phipps, aged eight, and in July inoculates him with smallpox, which does not take: one subject, no control, and a result the Royal Society declined to print. His Inquiry (1798) and the replies to it are public domain. Beside *A Dozen Healthy Infants* and *Two Oranges and a Lemon*.
- [open] **The Morris worm, 1988** — A Cornell graduate student releases a program on 2 November 1988 to measure the size of the Internet; a mistake in its rate of reinfection fills the machines it reaches, about 6,000 of them, within a day. The Cornell commission's report (1989), the GAO's report (1990) and the court record of United States v. Morris are open. Beside *The Nuclear Boy Scout*.
- [open] **Cold fusion, 1989** — Martin Fleischmann and Stanley Pons announced at a press conference at the University of Utah on 23 March 1989 that a palladium cell in heavy water gave off more heat than its current put in, and nuclear products with it; laboratories everywhere tried to repeat it through the spring, the neutron and gamma readings fell apart under scrutiny, and the Utah legislature had voted five million dollars before the year was out. The Energy Research Advisory Board's report to the Department of Energy (November 1989) is public domain; the papers are in copyright. Pons is living. Beside *An Aluminium Prism*.

## Results nobody read

*The site's recurring lesson*

- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-two-oranges-and-a-lemon.md] **James Lind, 1747** — The first controlled trial finds citrus cures scurvy; the Navy acts 48 years later, and Lind himself never quite believed it.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-eighteen-hundred-boxes.md] **Mincome and SIME/DIME, 1970s** — Guaranteed-income trials; the Manitoba data sat boxed for 30 years, and the US one was killed by a divorce finding later shown to be an artefact.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-thirty-eight-boys.md] **Cambridge-Somerville, 1939–45** — A mentoring programme for at-risk boys; thirty years on, the boys who got the help had done worse. Joan McCord's follow-up is the whole entry.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-chicken-heart.md] **Carrel's chicken heart, 1912–46** — Cells kept "immortal" for 34 years; after his death nobody could repeat it, and the likeliest reason is that the feed kept adding fresh cells.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-seventy-five-words.md] **Kon-Tiki, 1947** — The raft reaches Polynesia and proves the voyage possible; the hypothesis it was built to prove turns out to be wrong.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-a-million-observations.md] **Universe 25, 1968–72** — Calhoun's mouse utopia collapses; what he measured and what the public took from it are two different experiments.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-forty-two.md] **St. Matthew Island, 1944–66** — 29 reindeer put ashore become 6,000, then 42. No one set out to test carrying capacity, but nothing has tested it better. *Sixteen Reindeer* tells it in a paragraph; link to that.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-one-in-seventy-two.md] **Tacoma Narrows, 1940** — A bridge that oscillated from the day it opened, filmed and measured for four months by engineers working on a fix, and fell while they were working. The Federal Works Agency's 1941 report (Ammann, von Kármán, Woodruff) is a US government work; the famous film is not.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-fifty-three-degrees.md] **The O-rings, 1986** — The temperature data on the table the night before Challenger, and the chart that did not show it. The Rogers Commission report, with the Thiokol memos inside it, is public domain. The American twin of Chernobyl's test.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-twenty-five-watts.md] **The Hawthorne lamps, 1924–32** — Output rose whatever they did to the lighting, or so the legend says; the original data, re-analysed in 2011, show something smaller and stranger. Roethlisberger and Dickson (1939): check the renewal record before quoting at length.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-one-in-a-hundred-and-ten.md] **Project Cirrus, 1947** — Langmuir and Schaefer seed a hurricane off Georgia with dry ice; it turns and comes ashore at Savannah. The Army Signal Corps' final report and General Electric's records, largely government works. Beside Hatfield.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-twenty-four-feet.md] **Mesmer and the Franklin commission, 1784** — The first blinded trial: patients magnetised by a tree that was not, and not by one that was. The commission's report and its 1785 English translation, public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-seven-hundred-metres.md] **Vajont, 1963** — A hydraulic model of the slope built at Nove in 1961 showed the wave a landslide would throw over the dam; the engineers read it, set a safe reservoir level from it, and filled past it; on 9 October 1963 the mountain came down and the wave killed about 2,000 people below. Hendron and Patton's 1985 report for the US Army Corps of Engineers is a government work; the Italian commission, the trial at L'Aquila and Tina Merlin's articles in *L'Unità* are in Italian.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-the-silver-skin.md] **Eijkman's chickens, 1890–97** — Beriberi in chickens fed the hospital's polished rice, gone when the cook stopped the supply; Vorderman's survey of a hundred prisons on Java; and Eijkman reading his own result as a poison in the rice and its antidote in the husk, until Grijns read it again. The *Geneeskundig Tijdschrift voor Nederlandsch-Indië* and the Dutch press are open on Delpher, in Dutch; Eijkman's Nobel lecture of 1929.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-tape-two.md] **The Stanford tapes, 1971** — The guards were coached, the famous breakdown was by a prisoner's own account acted, and the recordings sat in the archive until 2018. Stanford's digitised collection is open; Le Texier's 2019 paper is open access; everything of Zimbardo's is in copyright and the argument is alive, so no villains and short quotations.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-chlorinated-lime.md] **Semmelweis, 1847** — Two maternity clinics in the Vienna General Hospital, one staffed by doctors who came from the dissecting room and one by midwives, with death rates of about one in ten and one in thirty; Semmelweis makes the doctors wash in chlorinated lime and the First Clinic's rate falls to the Second's. The result is refused by the profession and he dies in an asylum in 1865. Die Ätiologie (1861) is public domain in German; the English translations are not. Beside *Thirty-Eight Grains* and *Two Oranges and a Lemon*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-three-eggs.md] **Three eggs, 1911** — Wilson, Bowers and Cherry-Garrard walk to Cape Crozier in the Antarctic winter night to collect emperor penguin eggs, because an embryo of the most primitive bird might show feathers growing from scales and settle a question of evolution; three eggs survive the journey, and the Natural History Museum does not want them. The Worst Journey in the World (1922) and Wilson's reports are public domain; the museum's report on the embryos (1934) found nothing of the kind. Beside *Sixteen Reindeer*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-three-quarters-of-a-pint.md] **The Lanarkshire milk experiment, 1930** — Twenty thousand schoolchildren in 67 schools, half given three-quarters of a pint of milk a day for four months, to measure what milk does for growth; the teachers were allowed to adjust the allocation if it looked unbalanced, chose the poorer children for the milk, and the result could not be read. The Department of Health for Scotland's report (1930) is Crown copyright, expired; "Student"'s critique in Biometrika (1931) and Fisher and Bartlett's letter to Nature are the reading. Beside *Three Menus* and *Eighteen Hundred Boxes*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-sixty-two-volunteers.md] **Gallops Island, 1918** — In November 1918 the Navy and the Public Health Service tried to give influenza to 62 volunteers from the Deer Island brig: sprayed with the secretions of the sick, injected with their blood, breathed on by them; none caught it. Rosenau's report in JAMA (1919) and McCoy's series at Angel Island are public domain, and why it failed has never been settled. Beside *Building Number One* and *One Cubic Centimetre*.
- [open] **Tuskegee, 1932 to 1972** — The Public Health Service's study of untreated syphilis in about four hundred Black men in Macon County, Alabama, set up to run six months and run for forty years; penicillin became the standard treatment in 1947 and the men were kept from it, and the study's own reports in the medical journals were read by nobody who stopped it. The CDC's records of the study in the National Archives, the Ad Hoc Advisory Panel's report (1973) and the Senate hearings (1973) are public domain. Beside *Eleven Convicts*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-four-hundred-inches.md] **Three Mile Island, 1979** — On 28 March 1979 a relief valve stuck open and the control-room light that should have shown it showed only that the valve had been told to close; the operators, trusting the light, throttled the pumps that were saving the core. The Kemeny Commission's report and the NRC's Rogovin inquiry (1979 to 1980) are US government works, with the operators' testimony and the trace of every instrument. Beside *Thirty-Six Seconds*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-one-point-three-millimetres.md] **Hubble's null correctors, 1990** — The telescope's main mirror was ground to the wrong shape because a spacer in the instrument that measured it was 1.3 millimetres out of place; two other test instruments disagreed with it and their readings were set aside as the less accurate. The Allen report (NASA, November 1990) is public domain, with the test records. Beside *Fifty-Three Degrees*.
- [open] **The Patriot clock, 1991** (overnight: failed, see RUN-LOG) — The battery at Dhahran had run for 100 hours, and its clock, counting tenths of a second in binary, had drifted a third of a second, enough to put the incoming Scud outside the window it was looking in; 28 soldiers died in the barracks on 25 February 1991, and the Army had the warning and the fix two weeks earlier. The GAO's report (1992) is public domain. Beside *Fifty-Three Degrees*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-eight-percent.md] **Clever Hans, 1904 to 1907** — A horse in Berlin that answered arithmetic by tapping, examined by a commission of thirteen that found no trick, and then by the psychologist Oskar Pfungst, who found the horse read the questioner's head and stopped when the questioner did not know the answer. Pfungst's book (1907; English 1911) is public domain. Beside *An Aluminium Prism* and *Twenty-Four Feet*.
- [open] **Apollo 13's oxygen tank, 1968 to 1970** — Oxygen tank 2 of the service module had been dropped two inches in 1968, and when it would not drain after the countdown test in March 1970 its heaters were run for about eight hours on the pad's 65-volt supply through thermostatic switches rated for 28; the switches welded shut, the tank's temperature gauge read no higher than 80 degrees while the inside passed a thousand, and the wiring was bared; the tank burst 56 hours into the flight on 13 April 1970. The Cortright Review Board's report (NASA, June 1970) is public domain, on NTRS. Beside *Fifty-Four Tests* and *One Point Three Millimetres*.
- [open] **Columbia, 2003** — A piece of foam of about 1.7 pounds from the external tank's bipod ramp struck the left wing's leading edge 82 seconds after launch on 16 January 2003; foam had come off that ramp on earlier flights and been classed an acceptable risk, three requests from engineers for pictures of the wing from the Air Force's telescopes were turned down, and the orbiter broke up on re-entry on 1 February. The Columbia Accident Investigation Board's report (August 2003) is public domain. The mirror of *Fifty-Three Degrees*.
- [open] **Mars Climate Orbiter, 1998 to 1999** — Lockheed Martin's ground software gave the thruster firings in pound-force seconds and JPL's navigation software read them as newton-seconds, a factor of 4.45; the navigators watched the orbiter's path disagree with the predictions for months and raised it without a formal report, and on 23 September 1999 it passed Mars at about 57 kilometres instead of 226 and was lost. The Mishap Investigation Board's Phase I report (NASA, November 1999) is public domain. Beside *One Point Three Millimetres*.
- [open] **Ariane flight 501, 1996** — The first Ariane 5 broke up about 37 seconds after lift-off from Kourou on 4 June 1996 with the four Cluster satellites; its inertial reference system was running an alignment routine carried over from Ariane 4 that had no purpose after lift-off, the new rocket's faster path overflowed a 16-bit conversion in it, and the backup unit, running the same software, had failed the same way a few hundredths of a second earlier; the software had never been tested with Ariane 5's trajectory. The Inquiry Board's report (ESA and CNES, July 1996) is published free. Beside *One Point Three Millimetres* and *The Patriot clock*.
- [open] **Therac-25, 1985 to 1987** — AECL's radiotherapy machine, the first to rely on software alone for its interlocks, gave at least six patients massive overdoses in Georgia, Ontario, Washington and Texas; the operator at Tyler, Texas, saw "Malfunction 54", which the manual did not explain, and pressed P to proceed, as the machine allowed, and the fault was found by the hospital's physicist, Fritz Hager, not by the maker. Nancy Leveson and Clark Turner's account (IEEE Computer, 1993) is in copyright, with Leveson's longer version free on her site; the FDA's recall records are public. Living people. Beside *Four Hundred Inches*.
- [open] **Fukushima Daiichi, 2008 to 2011** — In 2008 TEPCO's own engineers computed that a tsunami of 15.7 metres could reach a plant built for 5.7; the figure was set aside as unproven, and the wave of 11 March 2011 came in at about 13 to 15 metres and drowned the diesel generators. The National Diet's Independent Investigation Commission report (2012) is published free in English, with the government's and TEPCO's own reports. Beside *Four Hundred Inches* and *Thirty-Six Seconds*.

## Whole systems run as trials

- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-two-hundred-feet.md] **Fordlandia, 1928–45** — Michigan transplanted to the Amazon; the rubber trees planted in rows catch the blight that scattered wild trees never do.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-three-million-acres.md] **The Groundnut Scheme, 1947–51** — Britain farms peanuts across Tanganyika without surveying the soil or the rainfall; tens of millions lost.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-five-thousand-numbers.md] **Project Cybersyn, 1971–73** — Stafford Beer wires Chile's factories to an operations room by telex; it works through a national strike and dies with the coup.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-twelve-stamps.md] **Wörgl, 1932–33** — An Austrian town issues money that loses value monthly; unemployment falls; the central bank shuts it down. (Close to Captain's Notes territory, so it may be a crossover.)
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-thirty-strings.md] **Starfish Prime and West Ford, 1961–63** — A 1.4-megaton test that put out Honolulu's streetlights and crippled a third of the satellites in orbit; half a billion copper needles flown as an artificial ionosphere.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-19-thirty-six-seconds.md] **Chernobyl's rundown test, 1986** — The disaster was itself a safety experiment, run at low power after a grid dispatcher delayed it into the night shift.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-twelve-beetles-a-night.md] **One hundred and two toads, 1935** — Cane toads shipped from Hawaii to Queensland against the cane beetle, bred and released before the trial was finished; Mungomery's own reports (Australian Crown copyright, expired) and the press on Trove. Beside Forty-Two.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-20-a-flying-pin.md] **Myxomatosis, 1950–51** — CSIRO's virus released against the rabbits works past all expectation, and when the public takes it for the encephalitis epidemic, Fenner, Burnet and Clunies Ross inject themselves on the record. Two experiments in one; CSIRO reports of that age are public domain.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-thirty-eight-grains.md] **Snow's two water companies, 1854** — The "grand experiment": 300,000 Londoners sorted by which company's pipe reached their house, and the death rate read off the pipe. On the Mode of Communication of Cholera (1855), public domain. Beside One Cubic Centimetre.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-one-in-a-hundred-thousand.md] **The swine flu programme, 1976** — One soldier dead at Fort Dix and thirteen sick become a plan to vaccinate every American; 45 million are, the pandemic never comes, and the instrument that spoke was the count of Guillain-Barré cases that stopped the programme in December. Neustadt and Fineberg's 1978 report for HEW, the CDC's *MMWR* and the congressional hearings are US government works; the 1976 press is not on archive.org.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-thirty-one-percent.md] **Project Stormfury, 1962–83** — Twenty years of seeding hurricanes with silver iodide, a "success" with Debbie in 1969, and the finding that ended it: unseeded storms did the same thing, and the supercooled water the theory needed was not there. NOAA's project plans and Willoughby and others' 1985 chronicle are US government works. Beside Project Cirrus and Hatfield.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-fifteen-megatons.md] **Castle Bravo, 1954** — The first dry hydrogen bomb was expected to yield six megatons and gave fifteen, because the designers had treated lithium-7 as inert; the fallout fell on Rongelap and on the Japanese boat Lucky Dragon, whose radio operator died. The instruments were the islands and ships downwind. The AEC's reports, the 1954 hearings and the Defense Nuclear Agency's history are public domain. Beside *Thirty Strings* and *Thirty-Six Seconds*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-rod-nine.md] **SL-1, 1961** — A small Army reactor in Idaho whose central control rod, lifted by hand during maintenance, could take it to prompt criticality alone; on 3 January 1961 it did, and the three men on top of it were the first reactor deaths in the United States. The AEC's investigation (1961 to 1962) and its film are public domain, and the design flaw had been written down for years. Beside *The Demon Core*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-seven-feet.md] **Windscale, 1957** — The Wigner energy in a graphite pile had to be released by heating it, a procedure done eight times before; on the ninth, in October 1957, the thermocouples were in the wrong places to see the fire, and the pile burned for two days. Penney's report, kept secret until 1988, is Crown copyright, expired. Beside *Thirty-Six Seconds*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-21-a-million-tons.md] **The Francis field trial and Cutter, 1954 to 1955** — The largest trial ever run, 1.8 million children, with the placebo arm injected with Salk's vaccine or salt water and the result read out at Ann Arbor on 12 April 1955; within weeks Cutter Laboratories' vaccine, released on the same tests, gave polio to about 40,000 children and killed ten. The trial's report (1957), the Public Health Service's technical report on the Cutter incident (1955) and Gottsdanker v. Cutter are open. Beside *Eighteen Hundred Boxes* and *One in a Hundred Thousand*.
- [published: _posts/2026-10-22-sixty-feet.md] **The Salton Sea, 1905 to 1907** — The California Development Company cut a new intake from the Colorado to feed the Imperial Valley without a headgate, because the gate would have taken a year; the river took the cut in 1905 and flowed into the Salton Sink for sixteen months until the Southern Pacific dammed it with trainloads of rock. Cory's account in the Transactions of the American Society of Civil Engineers (1913), the Reclamation Service's reports and Roosevelt's message to Congress (1907) are public domain. Beside *Four Billion Gallons*.
- [open] **The French canal, 1881 to 1889** — Ferdinand de Lesseps, fresh from Suez, cut a sea-level canal through Panama on the hypothesis that the isthmus was Suez with rain; the rain, the Chagres and yellow fever took about twenty thousand workers and the company went bankrupt. The French liquidation inquiry, the Isthmian Canal Commission's reports (1899 to 1901) and Bunau-Varilla's book (1913) are public domain. Beside *Three Million Acres* and *Two Hundred Feet*.
- [open] **Six prisoners, 1721** — With smallpox in London, the Princess of Wales had six condemned prisoners at Newgate inoculated on 9 August 1721 before the Royal Society, with pardons for the survivors; all six lived, one was then sent to sleep beside a smallpox patient, and the orphans of St James's parish were tried next. Maitland's account (1722) and the Philosophical Transactions are public domain. Beside *Eleven Convicts* and *A Dozen Healthy Infants*.
- [open] **Operation Sea-Spray, 1950** — From 20 to 27 September 1950 a Navy minesweeper off the Golden Gate sprayed Serratia marcescens and Bacillus globigii over San Francisco to measure how a city could be dosed from the sea; in the weeks after, Stanford's hospital saw eleven patients with Serratia infections, an organism it had never recorded, and one of them, Edward Nevin, died. The Army's test reports and the Senate health subcommittee's hearings of 1977 (Biological Testing Involving Human Subjects by the Department of Defense) are public domain; Wheat and others' paper (1951) described the outbreak without its cause. Beside *Thirty Strings* and *Eleven Convicts*.
- [open] **The Mississippi Basin Model, 1943 to 1993** — The Corps of Engineers built the whole river basin at Clinton, Mississippi, 200 acres at one in two thousand horizontal and one in a hundred vertical, largely with German prisoners of war, to try levees and reservoirs on the model before the river; in 1952 it ran the Missouri's flood ahead of the water and Omaha raised its levees to the model's numbers; after its last large run in 1973 it was wound down, closed in 1993 and left to the trees. The Corps' and the Waterways Experiment Station's reports are public domain. The counterexample: an instrument that was heard. Beside *Sixty Feet* and *Four Billion Gallons*.

## The instrument pointed at the wrong thing

*Frauds, not experiments, so not yet the shape of an entry. Each was a test of a check (a dipstick, an audit, an
assay, a sealed safe) run by someone who knew where it could not see, and the lesson is the site's own: the number
nobody read. If one is written, it wants a category of its own.*

- [open] **The salad oil swindle, 1955 to 1965** — Anthony "Tino" De Angelis's Allied Crude Vegetable Oil Refining Corporation borrowed against soybean and cottonseed oil in a tank farm at Bayonne, New Jersey, on warehouse receipts from a subsidiary of American Express whose inspectors dipped the tanks; the tanks held water under a layer of oil, with chambers under the hatches kept full of oil, and oil was pumped from tank to tank ahead of the inspectors; by 1963 the receipts claimed about 900,000 tons against some 55,000 in the tanks, more than the Census Bureau's monthly figure for the whole country's stock, and fifty-one lenders had advanced 150 to 180 million dollars. His corner in oil futures broke on 15 November 1963 when the Commodity Exchange Authority announced an inquiry and the Soviet wheat deal stalled; Allied was bankrupt within days, the brokerages Ira Haupt and Williston & Beane were suspended on 20 November, and De Angelis pleaded guilty and was sentenced to twenty years in 1965. Norman Miller's Wall Street Journal series (Pulitzer 1964) and his book The Great Salad Oil Swindle (1965) are in copyright, borrowable on archive.org; the reported court opinions of the collapse (the New York Court of Appeals on the receipts, 1965; the Second Circuit on Ira Haupt), the Commodity Exchange Authority's inquiry, the trustee's accounts and the Census Bureau's fats-and-oils reports are public domain. Beside *Twenty-Five Hundred* and *One Point Three Millimetres*.
- [open] **Ponzi, 1919 to 1920** — Charles Ponzi's Securities Exchange Company in Boston took in millions from December 1919 on a promise of fifty per cent in forty-five days from the trade in international postal reply coupons; in late July 1920 the Boston Post printed Clarence Barron's arithmetic, that the scheme would need about 160 million coupons in circulation when about 27,000 existed, and federal agents closed the office on 10 August with no coupons found. The Boston Post of 1920 (Pulitzer 1921) and the federal court record are public domain; Ponzi's own memoir (1935) needs a copyright check. Beside *Twenty-Five Hundred*.
- [open] **The Humbert safe, 1883 to 1903** — Thérèse Humbert borrowed for twenty years in Paris against the will of an American millionaire, Robert Crawford, whose bonds were said to lie in a safe sealed in her house on the avenue de la Grande-Armée; a court ordered it opened on 9 May 1902 and the papers reported a brick, an old newspaper and a button, or next to nothing; the family fled, was arrested in Madrid in December, and was tried before the Seine assizes in 1903, Thérèse getting five years. Le Figaro, Le Petit Journal and the trial reports of 1902 to 1903 are on Gallica, public domain; the Spectator of 24 May 1902 in English. Beside *Twenty-Four Feet*.
- [open] **Poyais, 1821 to 1824** — Gregor MacGregor, styling himself Cazique of Poyais, sold bonds and land of a country on the Mosquito Shore that existed only in his guidebook, Sketch of the Mosquito Shore (1822, under the name Thomas Strangeways); the Honduras Packet sailed from London in September 1822 and the Kennersley Castle from Leith in January 1823 with some 250 settlers, who found no town at the Black River, and most of them died there or at Belize. The Sketch, James Hastie's Narrative of a Voyage in the Kennersley Castle (1823) and the London papers of 1823 are public domain. Beside *Three Million Acres*.
- [open] **Ivar Kreuger, 1917 to 1932** — The Swedish match king lent governments money against match monopolies and financed it with paper that never balanced; after he shot himself in Paris on 12 March 1932 the auditors found forty-two forged Italian treasury bills of 500,000 pounds each, signed in the name of the Italian finance minister, carried on the books of Kreuger & Toll. Price Waterhouse's report and the testimony before the United States Senate Banking Committee in January 1933 are public domain; the Swedish inquiry is in Swedish. Beside *Twelve Stamps*.
- [open] **McKesson & Robbins, 1925 to 1938** — Philip Musica, a twice-convicted swindler who ran the drug wholesaler as F. Donald Coster, carried on its books a crude drug business with some ten million dollars of stock in Canadian warehouses and nineteen million in all of assets that existed only on paper; the auditors, Price Waterhouse, had never seen the warehouses, the company's own treasurer found it out in 1938, and Musica shot himself in December. The Securities and Exchange Commission's report of its investigation (1940) is public domain; the rule that auditors must see the stock and confirm the debts came from it. Beside *One Point Three Millimetres*.
- [open] **Equity Funding, 1964 to 1973** — A Los Angeles insurer sold reinsurers policies it had invented, more than forty thousand by 1972 and a third of its book, with a computer programme written to keep them out of the auditors' samples; a dismissed officer told the analyst Raymond Dirks in March 1973, the state insurance examiners impounded the records in April, and the Supreme Court's opinion in Dirks v. SEC (1983) tells the discovery. The trustee's report (1974) and the SEC's findings are public domain. Beside *One Point Three Millimetres* and *Four Hundred Inches*.
- [open] **Bre-X, 1993 to 1997** — A Calgary company's drill cores from Busang in Borneo assayed as what it called the largest gold deposit ever found, some 70 million ounces by its last count; Freeport-McMoRan drilled a metre and a half from Bre-X's holes in March 1997 and found "insignificant amounts of gold", the chief geologist Michael de Guzman fell from a helicopter on his way to meet them, and the Strathcona Mineral Services report of 4 May 1997 found the samples had been salted with alluvial gold before they reached the laboratory. The Strathcona report and the Ontario court's judgment acquitting John Felderhof (2007) are public; the Northern Miner's reporting is in copyright. Living people. Beside *An Aluminium Prism*.
- [open] **Madoff, 1992 to 2008** — Bernard Madoff's advisory business returned about one per cent a month for seventeen years on a "split-strike" strategy whose trades never happened; Harry Markopolos put the arithmetic to the Securities and Exchange Commission five times from 2000, and the SEC Inspector General's report (2009) on why the Commission never checked a single trade with a counterparty is public domain, with the Senate and House hearings. Recent; the victims are living. Beside *An Aluminium Prism* and *Four Hundred Inches*.
- [open] **Theranos, 2003 to 2018** — Elizabeth Holmes's Edison analyser was to run some two hundred tests on a drop of blood from a finger; it ran about a dozen, badly, the rest went through conventional machines on diluted samples, and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' inspection of the Newark, California laboratory in late 2015 found it an immediate danger to patients. The CMS inspection report (January 2016) and the trial record of United States v. Holmes (2021 to 2022) are public; John Carreyrou's reporting and book are in copyright. Recent; the people are living. The instrument itself was the thing that was not there. Beside *An Aluminium Prism*.
